Android apps, at home on your Mac.

Feels native

Not an emulator window. Your apps.

A window per app

Each app gets its own Android display streamed into its own Mac window. Resize it and the app re-lays itself out like on a tablet.

Install by double-click

Open an .apk, .apks or .xapk and it installs, appears in Launchpad and opens. Apps installed inside Android show up too.

Notifications

Android notifications land in Notification Centre. Click one and the app opens in its window.

Copy and paste

Copy in Android, paste on the Mac, and the other way around. Your Mac keyboard and layout just work.

Game controllers

Connect an Xbox, PlayStation or Switch controller to your Mac and Android games pick it up automatically.

Open source

GPLv3, built on LineageOS, QEMU and scrcpy. No account, no Google services, nothing leaves your Mac.

Always ready

Starts with your Mac. Opens apps instantly.

Turn on Always Ready and the Subsystem starts when you log in and keeps Android running in the background, so an Android app opens as fast as any Mac app. In Smart mode Android uses almost nothing while it waits, and gets out of the way whenever your Mac is busy.

  • Lives in the menu bar: status, modes, your apps, settings and Quit.
  • Prefer it lighter? As needed starts Android only when you open an app and turns it off when you're done. In Power Saver mode, Android never runs without an app open.
  • Quitting turns Android off cleanly.

Game controllers

Plug in a controller. Play.

Any controller your Mac recognises (Xbox, PlayStation DualSense and DualShock, Switch Pro, MFi) shows up in Android as a standard gamepad that every Android game understands. No setup, no button mapping.

  • Sticks, analog triggers, d-pad and every button.
  • Up to eight controllers for local multiplayer.
  • The controller drives whichever Android window is in front.
How it works

Five layers, one seamless app.

Every piece is open source. The guide goes into detail.

Your Android appsMac apps in ~/Applications/Android Apps, each one its own process with its own Dock icon.
App windowsEach window asks Android for a new virtual display and streams it with hardware video decoding.
The SubsystemA menu bar app that starts and stops Android, installs apps, applies the performance mode and relays notifications.
Android 17LineageOS 24 without Google apps, built for virtual machines, about 1.5 GB to download.
Apple siliconQEMU on Apple's Hypervisor framework: Arm Android on an Arm Mac at close to native speed.
Get started

Four steps, about five minutes.

  1. Install

    Open the DMG and drag Mac Subsystem for Android to Applications.

  2. Set up Android

    It downloads Android for you. Tick Always Ready if you want apps to open instantly.

  3. Add apps

    Double-click APKs, or get F-Droid and install from there.

  4. Open them

    From Launchpad, Spotlight or the Dock, like any other app.

Questions

Good to know.

Who makes it?

It's an independent, open-source project. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Google, Apple or LineageOS.

Can I use Google Play?

Not out of the box. The Android image has no Google apps, so you install apps as APKs or from F-Droid. Apps that require Google Play services may not work.

Does it drain my battery?

In Power Saver mode Android only runs while an Android app is open, and turns off 30 seconds after you close the last one. Smart mode keeps Android ready, but on the efficiency cores with most of its memory handed back to macOS whenever no Android app is in front, and it drops to power saving whenever the battery is low or other apps need your Mac.

Will it fill up my disk?

It cleans up after itself: finished downloads, install leftovers, old images and logs go automatically, and space Android frees is given back to your Mac. When your Mac is low on space it also clears Android apps' caches and compacts Android's disks. Settings › Storage shows what it uses.

Which controllers work?

Anything macOS's Game Controller framework supports: Xbox Wireless, PlayStation DualSense and DualShock 4, Nintendo Switch Pro and MFi controllers. Pair them in System Settings › Bluetooth.

Will it run on my Intel Mac, iPhone or iPad?

No. It needs Apple silicon for the hypervisor, and iOS doesn't allow apps to run virtual machines like this.

Can developers use it?

Yes. Turn on Developer mode and connect with adb connect 127.0.0.1:58526. Android Studio sees it like a device.
